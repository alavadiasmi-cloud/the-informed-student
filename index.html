Why this matters

Politics is already shaping the world around you.

The policies governments make affect education, technology, climate, jobs, rights, trade and the conflicts that shape international relations. Being interested does not mean agreeing with a particular side—it means understanding what is happening, asking questions and being able to form your own view from evidence.

Good political awareness also means being careful about where information comes from. UNESCO describes media and information literacy as the ability to engage critically with information and navigate the online environment safely. Reliable reporting, primary documents and multiple perspectives can help you separate what is known from what is claimed.

Source: UNESCO — Media and Information Literacy

Politics, explained

Ask about the world around you.

Choose a topic or ask the explainer below. The built-in answers are deliberately based on a small set of curated institutional sources rather than invented facts.

How does the UN work?
The UN is an international organisation whose member states cooperate on issues including peace and security, development and human rights. Different UN bodies have different powers and responsibilities.
What is soft power?
Soft power refers to influencing others through attraction and persuasion rather than coercion or payment. It is commonly discussed alongside hard power and, in some frameworks, as part of a broader strategy called smart power.
Why do states go to war?
There is no single explanation. International-relations theories examine factors such as security dilemmas, power, interests, institutions, domestic politics and ideas. Different theories emphasise different causes.
What is the Security Council veto?
The UN Security Council has five permanent members. A negative vote by one of those members can prevent adoption of a substantive resolution, even when most other members support it.
How does international law work?
International law governs relations among states and other actors through treaties, customary rules and institutions. Courts such as the International Court of Justice hear particular types of disputes under defined legal rules.
What makes a news source reliable?
Look for identifiable reporting, evidence, transparent sourcing, corrections, clear separation between news and opinion, and corroboration from other credible sources. A social-media post can point you toward a story, but it should not automatically be treated as the evidence itself.
